02 · Disciplines

The discipline changes the mechanism — and the consequence.

The three categories belong in one portfolio, but not in one denominator. Comparisons must preserve environment, exposure and injury definition.

01 rope + bolts

Sport climbing

Protected falls, belay errors, communication failures, knot errors and ground impact. Indoor and outdoor should be separated whenever possible.

Published signal: in the Swedish study, 84% of sport-climbing injuries were associated with falls; 31% involved rope-management errors by the belayer.

02 no rope · landing mat

Bouldering

The fall is short but repeated and concentrated on the ankle, foot and knee. The height of the problem is not only on the wall: it is also in the landing.

Published signal: 89% of Swedish bouldering injuries occurred after falls; a German hospital study found 61.1% lower-limb injuries.

03 mobile protection · pitches

Traditional / multi-pitch

Protection placement, anchors, descent and rappel, communication and prolonged exposure. One label can hide single-pitch and long routes.

Published signal: all traditional-climbing injuries in the Swedish sample occurred after a fall.

04 · Analytical plan

From narrative to comparable patterns.

The portfolio is an editorial front-end; the auditable inventory stays behind it with source lineage, inclusion rules and coverage limits.

01 · Discover

Map sources

Country, system, licence, period and unit.

02 · Classify

Separate discipline

Sport, bouldering, traditional; indoor/outdoor.

03 · Code

Describe event

Mechanism, cause, severity, injury and outcome.

04 · Reconcile

Deduplicate

Original source, derivative, person, event and publication.

05 · Compare

Interpret

Counts and rates only when the denominator supports them.

What we will not do: add NEISS, AAC/ANAC, hospitals, voluntary reports and near misses into a worldwide total. That is a denominator mixture wearing statistical clothing.
05 · Sources and traceability

Where the numbers came from.

Each quantitative result is tied to an identifiable source. Accident, hospital and contextual sources are not treated as the same population.
